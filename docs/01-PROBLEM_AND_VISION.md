# 01 — Problem, Idea, and Vision

## The problem
Early-stage founders often collect scattered interview notes, survey results, feature requests, and product observations. Insights are difficult to compare over time and frequently lose their connection to the original customer evidence. Teams can end up building what a few vocal customers request instead of learning which segment gets durable value.

Existing analytics and survey tools answer useful but disconnected questions: what users did, what users said, and whether they would miss the product. A founder still has to connect these signals to a defensible product decision.

This is the motivating **hypothesis**, not yet an independently validated market finding.

## The idea
A personal PMF Engine: a private workspace where a founder can map a target market, record customer discoveries, measure dependence through the Sean Ellis survey, derive source-linked insights, propose hypotheses, run experiments, and record decisions.

**Core learning chain:** Customer → Original evidence → Insight → Hypothesis → Experiment → Learning.

## Who it is for first
One founder/product owner evaluating one or more software product concepts. V1 is explicitly for personal use; no team permissions, agency workspaces, public user accounts, or subscription plans.

The system should work before launch (interviews and problem validation) and after launch (surveys and deliberate product experiments).

## Jobs to be done
- Preserve what customers actually said or did instead of relying on memory.
- Identify the customer segment that values the product most.
- Quantify a qualified group's response to losing access to the product.
- Recognize core benefits and blockers without treating every request equally.
- Choose the smallest useful validation experiment and keep its outcome attached to the hypothesis.
- Revisit conclusions when contradictory evidence appears.

## Non-goals for V1
No promises to mathematically "prove" PMF. No universal synthetic PMF score, automatic AI product manager, customer billing, external analytics sync, real-time tracking, or enterprise dashboard.

## Success criteria for the personal tool
A founder can complete one end-to-end evidence-to-decision cycle without spreadsheets or disconnected documents; understand where each conclusion came from; compute PMF with the correct sample/segment; and retrieve past experiments and their outcomes.

## Future potential
After personal utility is demonstrated, consider product analytics, invoices/revenue signals, assisted tagging, notification workflows, collaboration, and integrations. These are hypotheses for later phases, not committed MVP scope.
